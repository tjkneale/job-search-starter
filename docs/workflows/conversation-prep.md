# Conversation preparation

Read the person/purpose/date/duration supplied by the user, existing relationship history, company/role records, candidate evidence, and story bank. Ask only for missing context that changes preparation, and continue independent research. Verify changing person/company facts; label hypotheses about influence or role ownership.

Use [the prep template](../../templates/conversation-prep.md). Lead with a quick review: purpose, opening, strongest proof points, priority questions, and intended close. Informational conversations emphasize perspective and fit calibration; recruiter screens add through-line, scope, gaps, location/compensation/timing and process; employer interviews connect the mandate to defensible stories, judgment, and likely follow-ups.

Select a few relevant stories without changing their facts. Use story development when an episode needs substantial work. Keep unresolved questions and disclosure boundaries visible. Do not force a whole new company audit when current research is sufficient.

Save or update a dated brief in `workspace/interview/`, linking it from the role or preparation index. Keep scheduled meeting date, preparation date, and observation dates separate. A brief is complete when the user can review it quickly and material claims have sources; it proves no attendance or pipeline movement.
